The Locker

Everything you have ever bookmarked on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube, stowed in one ledger. Request the export, drop the file, and get subject, description, category and the original link for every saved post — three platforms merged into one table, tagged automatically and exported in one click.

Runs in your browser

Step 1 — Request the export

Open Instagram export →

accountscenter.instagram.com/info_and_permissions/dyi

Not under Your Activity. The export lives in Accounts Center, a different area of settings. If you land on a page listing profile changes — phone number, bio, privacy — you are in Your Activity → Account history and the export is not there. Go back to Settings and open Accounts Center at the top.

Gives you: permalink, date saved, and the full caption. Ready in about an hour.

  1. Sign in if prompted, then choose Download or transfer information
  2. Pick your Instagram account, then Some of your information — not All available information, which pulls every photo you have ever posted
  3. Scroll to the Your Instagram activity group and tick Saved posts. It sits inside that group, not at the top level, and is easy to scroll past. Add Likes and Collections if you want them logged too.
  4. Choose Download to device, date range All time, and switch format to JSON — it defaults to HTML on some accounts, and HTML carries no dates or account names
  5. Submit, then watch for Meta's email
  6. Come back here and drop the zip below. No need to unzip it.
Open TikTok export →

tiktok.com/setting/download-your-data

Watch the clock on this one. The file takes one to four days to build, and TikTok's own terms allow up to thirty. Once it is ready the download link expires after four days. Miss that window and you start the request over.

Looking for bookmarks? TikTok calls them Favorites. There is no category by any other name.

Gives you: link and date only — no account name, no caption.

  1. Open the Request data tab. In the app the path is Profile → ☰ → Settings and privacy → Account → Download your data.
  2. Hit Deselect all, then tick Likes and Favorites — TikTok has no category called bookmarks or saved, and this is the one that holds them
  3. Leave Your Activity unticked despite the name. That is watch history, search history, ad interests and Off-TikTok activity — a large file with nothing for the ledger in it.
  4. Scroll down past the category list and set file format to JSON. This matters more than on Instagram — the TXT export carries a fraction of the data and cannot be parsed.
  5. Request data, then check the Download data tab over the next few days
  6. Drop the zip below. Favorite Videos and Like List are both picked up.
Open Google Takeout →

takeout.google.com/settings/takeout

Watch Later does not export. Google treats it as a system playlist and leaves it out of Takeout, and there is no resource for it in the portability schema either. The workaround is to open Watch Later, add the videos to an ordinary playlist, and export that. Liked videos have the same problem.

Gives you: video ID and the date added, per playlist. The playlist name becomes the subject.

  1. Click Deselect all at the top — Takeout ticks every Google product by default
  2. Scroll to YouTube and YouTube Music and tick it
  3. Open All YouTube data included and reduce it to playlists — leave out history and videos unless you want a very large file
  4. Next step → delivery by download link, one-off export, .zip
  5. Create export, then wait for the email. Large accounts can take hours.
  6. Drop the zip below. Each playlist CSV becomes a group of rows.
Why none of these is fully automatic. None of the three platforms exposes your saves, favorites or playlists to an API that another app can read on your behalf. The export download link only works inside your own logged-in session. Any tool claiming one-click retrieval is driving your logged-in browser, which breaks the platform's terms and puts the account at risk. These buttons remove every step that can be removed; the automation starts once the file is in hand.

Step 2 — Drop the file

Drop the export here The whole .zip from any of the three, or the loose files if you have already unzipped it. Drop more than one and they merge into a single ledger. Everything is parsed on your own machine — no upload, no server, no account needed. saved_posts.json · liked_posts.json · user_data.json · playlists .csv

Re-import any time. New posts are added, and every subject and description you have written is kept.

Subject matter rules

Each rule matches text in the caption, an account handle, a link or a collection name, and fills in the subject matter wherever they are still blank. Rules run on every import, so once they are set the next export tags itself.

What each export actually carries. Instagram now ships the full caption alongside the link and the date, so its rows arrive already written — first line as the subject, whole caption as the description. TikTok ships a link and a date, nothing else. YouTube ships a video ID and the date you added it. For those two, subject and description come from your rules, your own typing, or the Fill descriptions round trip, which hands the links to an AI session with web access and merges the answers back by post ID.
